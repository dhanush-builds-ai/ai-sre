### R1 — High-entropy redaction erases the evidence the platform needs
- **Where:** SC-09, log Q5.26.
- **Problem:** "High-entropy strings" also match commit SHAs (`a1b2c3…`), image digests, trace and span IDs, and config content hashes. These are the evidence that ties incident A to config v42, and SC-14 binds approvals to exactly these hashes. Redacting them breaks diagnosis and approval binding.
- **Proposed fix:** Before the entropy rule, keep values in known ID formats **from known fields** (commit SHA and image digest in deploy history, trace and span IDs in trace context, config version IDs and content hashes in config history). Add to ST-12: after redaction, fixture A evidence still shows the commit, the config version and the trace IDs.

### R2 — Webhook signature assumes every monitoring tool can sign
- **Where:** SC-02, log Q5.6 and Q5.21, ST-05.
- **Problem:** HMAC signing is not universal. Some alert senders support only a bearer token or basic auth over TLS. No assumption records this. A signature without a timestamp can also be replayed.
- **Proposed fix:** SC-02: "a valid HMAC signature, or, where the sender cannot sign, a per-source secret token over TLS; signed requests carry a timestamp and are rejected outside a 5-minute window." Add **L5-A7**: "Each V1 alert source supports a signature or a per-source token" — owner Dhanush, validate in Layer 6 (technology selection). A replayed alert is also grouped by FR-02, so the impact is low.
- [x] Approve
- **Your change:**
