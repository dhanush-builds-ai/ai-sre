4.2Description

Answers: Q4.1 to Q4.34

A description says what the system must do, in one or two sentences, with "must". It does not say how to build it. It refers to the earlier decision where one exists (for example A1 E6).

Figure 1. Functional requirements on the incident flow. Recovery (revert or config restore) comes before the fix phase.

4.4Measurement

Answers: Q4.1 to Q4.34; the values come from Q4.16 to Q4.34

A measurement names the observable quantity: a state, a count, a duration, a percentage, or "present / absent". Every measured value comes from the platform's own metrics (NFR-11) or from the audit record (FR-15).

Figure 2. Nested limits for one incident. Each limit stops the work and escalates to a human.

4.5Acceptance criterion

Answers: Q4.1 to Q4.34

An acceptance criterion is a pass or fail statement about the measurement. A requirement passes only when its criterion is true in its validation method. A requirement with no testable criterion is not a requirement yet (worksheet focus).

4.6Validation method

Answers: Q4.1 to Q4.34

MethodWhat it is
Fixture testRun a synthetic incident (A, B, C from Artifact 3, or a seeded variant) end to end, and check the outcome.
Fault testInject a failure during a fixture run: stop a worker, the database, intake, or slow a source.
Load testSend many incidents or requests at the same time, and measure.
Audit checkInspect the audit record and timeline for required entries, or for forbidden actors.
Leak scanSearch stored evidence, prompts, and outputs for seeded secrets and personal data.
DrillA planned exercise, for example a database restore, measured against the target.
